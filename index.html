Brand Audit

Ask a company what its brand stands for and you get the story. Look at what it puts into the market and you get the brand.

The same gap we study in customers shows up in companies. The brand framework says one thing; the accumulated output, from the website and social feeds to press and reviews, often says another. We audit brands from the outside in, the way a prospective customer actually meets them, and every finding carries its evidence: the quote, the source, the date.

The outside-in audit

No documents needed. We reconstruct your brand framework from everything you have put into the market and show where it agrees with itself and where it does not. A recent audit logged 105 verbatim quotes across 18 sources; findings a team can argue with, but not wave away.

The gap analysis

Bring your brand framework and we compare it to what the market actually sees, element by element. The most valuable findings are usually the ones a team cannot see from inside.

Brand standards

The audit becomes a working standard your team can check any asset against before it goes to market, so the same decisions do not have to be made twice.

AI Discovery

Before AI can elevate your business, organize what you know.

AI Discovery is the newest use of our oldest skill. Most organizations know they should be using AI but have no credible way to choose where or how. The hard part was never the technology. It is discovery.

The elevating starts with the groundwork: before AI can help, a company needs what it knows, its brand promise, rules, terminology, and data, organized into a system AI can actually use. Most of that lives in people's heads, and getting it out faithfully is what research does.

The inside lens: the work

Process docs and self-reports are stories people tell about their own habits. The real workflow, where the hours and friction go, has to be discovered. We map what is actually done, by whom, how, and when; which parts people would be glad to hand off; and what their freed time gets spent on instead. Automation only counts as a win when the reclaimed hours move to judgment, relationships, and creativity.

The outside lens: the customer

Customers judge a brand in seconds, on feeling, before any reasoning happens. When someone wants a human and gets a phone tree or a chatbot, the brand pays for it at that gut level, and no argument about efficiency wins them back. So every automation that touches the customer gets tested against the brand promise before it gets built.

Hours saved at the brand's expense are borrowed, not earned.

01

Start where you are

Every engagement begins with the client: where you are with AI today, what has been tried, and which areas of the business make the most sense to explore first.

02

Discover

We'll map the workflows, tools, and knowledge by collaborating with your teams to learn where the time, friction, and rework go, and importantly what the team would be glad to automate.

03

Prioritize

Score the work itself, the tasks and workflows, never the people, through several different lenses, including impact, feasibility, human fit, and brand fit. The output is a short list: not forty ideas, but the few tasks worth doing and the order to do them in.

04

Roadmap

An executive-ready plan defining your options to automate: what each would require, the expected return, and how they could be sequenced. When you are ready, we can help you pilot a single automation and measure the return with real before-and-after numbers.

The right automation takes the repetitive, low-value work off your team so their time goes where judgment, relationships, and creativity matter most. That is what makes adoption stick, and it is what keeps the brand promise intact.

The companies that win with AI will not have the best models. They will have the best-organized understanding of their own work, and that is research.
