How we work

From problem to method.

The interviewing, analysis, and judgment are the same craft whatever we point them at: brand positioning, segmentation, pricing, innovation, customer understanding, and lately, AI discovery. Whatever the question, the way we work stays the same.

Start with the problem

Clients rarely arrive with a research brief. They arrive with a problem or a decision they need to make. Our first job is collaborating to understand the business question and any constraints. Only then do we discuss approaches, because budgets and timelines are real, and we design to them as carefully as we design the study.

Research the right market

Answering the question starts with determining who we need to research to answer it, then making sure we are really reaching those people. Bots and professional respondents have made that second part a craft of its own. Our field partners and quality checks carry that weight.

Customized deliverables

The deliverable is customized to your needs and audience, whether that is an executive presentation, a technical readout, a working session, or an interactive report or market simulator your team keeps using after we leave.

AI Discovery

Before AI can elevate your business, build the foundation.

AI Discovery is the newest use of our oldest skill. Most organizations know they should be using AI but have no credible way to choose where or how. The hard part was never the technology. It is discovery.

Before choosing tools or building workflows, a company needs to extract the knowledge about its brand promise and processes for AI to use. Most of that lives in people's heads, and getting it out faithfully is what research does.

The inside lens: the work

Process docs and self-reports are stories people tell about their own habits. The real workflow, where the hours and friction go, has to be discovered. We map what is actually done, by whom, how, and when; and which parts people would be glad to hand off. Automation only counts as a win when the reclaimed hours move to judgment, relationships, and creativity.

The outside lens: the customer

Customers judge a brand in seconds, on feeling, before any reasoning happens. When someone wants a human and gets a phone tree or a chatbot, the brand pays for it at that gut level, and no argument about efficiency wins them back. So every automation that touches the customer gets tested against the brand promise before it gets built.

Hours saved at the brand's expense are borrowed, not earned.

01

Start where you are

Every engagement begins with the client: where you are with AI today, what has been tried, and which areas of the business make the most sense to explore first.

02

Discover

We'll map the workflows, tools, and knowledge by collaborating with your teams to learn where the time, friction, and rework go, and importantly what the team would be glad to automate. Along the way, we'll also flag the data and tools each task involves, so any build starts with known-requirements.

03

Prioritize

We'll evaluate tasks and workflows (never people) through several different lenses, including impact, feasibility, human fit, and brand fit, and collaborate with you to determine which tasks to prioritize for AI development.

04

Roadmap

An executive-ready plan defining your options to automate: what each would require, the expected return, and how they could be sequenced. When you are ready, we can help you pilot a single automation and measure the return with real before-and-after numbers.

The right automation takes the repetitive, low-value work off your team so their time goes where judgment, relationships, and creativity matter most. That is what makes adoption stick, and it is what keeps the brand promise intact.

The companies that win with AI will not have the best models. They will have the best-organized understanding of their own work, and that is research.

Heather Corts, Founder

Corts Research was founded by Heather Corts. Over 25 years she has designed and led custom qualitative and quantitative studies, across both B2B and consumer markets, for brands from CPG to healthcare to apparel to consumer technology.

Behind every study sits a conviction borrowed from behavioral science. Most decisions are made fast, on intuition and feeling; the reasons people give come later, and they are stories. The gap between the two is why research exists, and why we design studies that catch how people decide rather than how they say they decide.
