Defining the question. Informing the decision.

Corts Research is a market research practice that helps companies answer the difficult questions behind a real business decision, across both B2B and consumer markets. Founded and led by Heather Corts, the practice brings 25 years of designing and leading custom qualitative and quantitative studies, work that began at Research International in New York.

What sets our work apart is where it starts. Not with a method, but with the decision a client is trying to make and what they need to learn to make it. From there we draw on a full mixed-methods toolkit, using in-depth qualitative through advanced analytics like conjoint, MaxDiff, and latent-class segmentation, to deliver synthesis clients can act on. Clients do not get a data dump. They get a clear read they can use.

Behind every study sits a conviction borrowed from behavioral science. Most decisions are made fast, on intuition and feeling; the reasons people give come later, and they are stories. Kahneman called the two modes System 1 and System 2, and the gap between them is why research exists. Ask a customer why they chose and you hear the story, not the decision. So we design research that catches how people decide, not how they say they decide.

We maintain strong relationships with field partners for consumer and professional respondents worldwide, and do our best work where the research drives a tangible business impact. Corts Research, L.L.C. is based in Nashville, Tennessee.

How we work

Decision-first, then the method.

Start with the decision

Before a proposal exists, the first question is what decision the client needs to make and what must be found out to inform it. Qualitative versus quantitative is a deliberate call, made before pricing. And because every decision comes with real constraints, we design to the budget and the timeline as deliberately as we design the method.

Define the market, then verify it

Good findings depend on who is answering. That starts with defining the market: who the research must include for the answer to hold, whether that is a hard-to-reach B2B decision-maker or a specific consumer segment. And it ends with verification, because in an era of bots and professional respondents, knowing your results came from the people you defined is no longer a given. Our trusted field partners and quality checks make sure they did.

Synthesis over transcription

The deliverable is the takeaway, not the transcript, and there is no house template. We work with each client on what the findings need to accomplish, who needs to hear them, and how they will land best. Every report is built for that audience, that question, and that decision.

Built for how people decide

Most choices are made fast, by feel; the explanations arrive after, dressed up as reasons. Our studies are designed to separate the behavior from the story, so decisions rest on what people do, not what they report. Fast choosers reach for the brand that comes to mind first, which is why the evidence base is how brands grow: mental availability (easy to think of), physical availability (easy to buy), and brand distinctiveness (unmistakably you), not folklore about the average customer.

AI Discovery

AI should never become a barrier. It should be a way to elevate.

Most organizations know they should be using AI but have no credible way to choose where. The hard part was never the technology. It is discovery: understanding what people really do all day, which of that work is worth automating, and how this may impact the customer experience. That is a research problem, and it is the work we have done for 25 years.

The inside lens: the work

Process docs and self-reports are stories people tell about their own habits. The real workflow, where the hours and friction go, has to be discovered, not surveyed. We map what is actually done, by whom, how, and when; which parts people would be glad to hand off; and what their freed time gets spent on instead. Automation only counts as a win when the reclaimed hours move to judgment, relationships, and creativity.

The outside lens: the customer

Customers judge a brand in seconds, on feeling, before any reasoning happens. When someone wants a human and gets a phone tree or a chatbot, the brand pays for it at that gut level, and no argument about efficiency wins them back. So every automation that touches the customer gets tested against the brand promise before it gets built.

The Four-Lens Test

Every candidate task is scored on four lenses before anything is built. Most AI consultants stop at the first two. The fourth is market research, and it is ours.

Impact

What automating this returns in hours, cost, errors, and capacity unlocked.

Feasibility

Whether the data, context, and process clarity exist to build it well.

Human fit

Whether the people doing the work would be glad to hand it off, and whether their time goes somewhere higher.

Brand fit

Whether it touches the customer, and whether it keeps the brand promise when it does.

AI only works if it strengthens the brand promise. Hours saved at its expense are borrowed, not earned.

01

Start where you are

Every engagement begins with the client: where you are with AI today, what has been tried, and which areas of the business make the most sense to explore first.

02

Discover

Then the research: interview the people who do the work and map how it really happens, where the time, friction, and rework go, what they would be glad to hand off, and which of it your customers can feel.

03

Prioritize

Score the work itself, the tasks and workflows, never the people, on all four lenses. The output is a short list: not forty ideas, but the few tasks worth doing and the order to do them in.

04

Roadmap

An executive-ready plan defining your options to automate: what each would require, the expected return, and how they could be sequenced. When you are ready, we can help you pilot a single automation and measure the return with real before-and-after numbers.

The point is to elevate people, not replace them, and to serve customers, not screen them. The right automation takes the repetitive, low-value work off your team so their time goes where judgment, relationships, and creativity matter most. That is what makes adoption stick, and it is what keeps the brand promise intact.

The companies that win with AI will not have the best models. They will have the best-organized understanding of their own work, and that is research.
